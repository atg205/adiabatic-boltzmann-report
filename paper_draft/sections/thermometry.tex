\section{Pooled conditional thermometry}
\label{sec:thermometry}

\subsection{RBM wavefunction and sampling model}
\label{sec:setup}

We use Pauli operators $\sigma_i^\mu$, $\mu\in\{x,y,z\}$, and computational-basis configurations $v=(v_1,\dots,v_N)$, $v_i=\pm1$. The transverse-field Ising chain,
\begin{equation}
H_{\mathrm I}=-J\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i^z\sigma_{i+1}^z-\Gamma\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i^x,
\label{eq:ising}
\end{equation}
with $J=1$ and periodic boundaries, supplies an exactly solvable reference energy for even $N$~\cite{Lieb1961,He2017} against which the validation procedure of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} is checked.

An RBM with $N$ visible spins $v$, $M$ hidden spins $h$, biases $(a,b)$, and couplings $W$ defines the auxiliary energy $\mathcal E_\theta(v,h)=\sum_i a_iv_i-\sum_jb_jh_j-\sum_{ij}W_{ij}v_ih_j$. Writing $\phi_j(v)=b_j+\sum_iW_{ij}v_i$, the marginal amplitude, Born distribution, and hidden conditional means are
\begin{align}
A_\theta(v)&=e^{-\frac12\sum_ia_iv_i}\prod_{j=1}^M[2\cosh\phi_j(v)]^{1/2},\label{eq:amplitude}\\
\pi_\theta(v)&=A_\theta(v)^2/Z_\theta,\qquad \psi_\theta(v)=s(v)\sqrt{\pi_\theta(v)},\label{eq:born}\\
\mathbb E[h_j\mid v]&=\tanh\phi_j(v).\label{eq:condmean}
\end{align}
The sign $s(v)$ is fixed (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}); it plays no role in this section. Parameters are updated with stochastic reconfiguration (SR) using the local energy $\Eloc(v)$ and the logarithmic derivatives of $A_\theta$~\cite{Sorella1998,Sorella2001}, exactly as in prior work on this system; we do not modify the optimizer and record it here only to fix notation for Sec.~\ref{sec:resources}.

A quantum annealer used as a sampler returns joint configurations $(v^{(r)},h^{(r)})_{r=1}^S$ at each SR iteration. If these were exact samples from the Gibbs distribution $q(v,h)\propto e^{-\betahw\mathcal E_\theta(v,h)}$ at some device inverse temperature $\betahw$, rescaling the submitted coefficients by $1/\beta_x$ would give an effective inverse temperature $\betaeff=\betahw/\beta_x$, and the corresponding visible marginal would be
\begin{equation}
\pi_{\theta,\beta}(v)=\frac{e^{-\beta a\cdot v}\prod_j2\cosh[\beta\phi_j(v)]}{Z_{\theta,\beta}}.
\label{eq:tempered}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:tempered} is a model to be tested against the sampler's actual output, not an identity that quantum annealing satisfies by construction.

\subsection{Least-squares pooled CEM}
\label{sec:cem-ls}

The pooled least-squares estimator reuses the $(v^{(r)},h^{(r)})$ pairs already produced by SR -- no fixed-condition re-query of the device is needed -- and fits
\begin{equation}
\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{LS}}=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta>0}\;
\mathcal L(\beta)=\sum_{r=1}^S\sum_{j=1}^M\big[h_j^{(r)}-\tanh(\beta\phi_j(v^{(r)}))\big]^2,
\label{eq:cem-ls}
\end{equation}
minimized by bounded scalar search over $\beta\in[0.01,50]$ in the implementation we audit. This is the estimator used in the source report and is the one for which Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration} reports residual visible-distribution error.

\subsection{Conditional maximum likelihood}
\label{sec:cem-mle}

Equation~\eqref{eq:cem-ls} treats each hidden bit's conditional distribution as though it were Gaussian around $\tanh(\beta\phi_j)$; the correct conditional model under Eq.~\eqref{eq:tempered} is Bernoulli. The conditional log-likelihood of the pooled hidden observations given the visible configurations, under a single inverse temperature $\beta$, is
\begin{equation}
\ell(\beta)=\sum_{r=1}^S\sum_{j=1}^M\Big[\beta\,h_j^{(r)}\Theta_j^{(r)}-\log\big(2\cosh(\beta\Theta_j^{(r)})\big)\Big],
\label{eq:cem-mle}
\end{equation}
where $\Theta_j^{(r)}\equiv\phi_j(v^{(r)})=b_j+\sum_kv_k^{(r)}W_{kj}$. The conditional maximum-likelihood estimator $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}=\arg\max_\beta\ell(\beta)$ solves the score equation
\begin{equation}
\sum_{r,j}\Theta_j^{(r)}\Big[h_j^{(r)}-\tanh\!\big(\beta\Theta_j^{(r)}\big)\Big]=0.
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:score} differs from the stationarity condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cem-ls} by the weight $\Theta_j^{(r)}$ multiplying each residual instead of a uniform weight; the two estimators therefore agree only in special cases (e.g. all $|\Theta_j^{(r)}|$ equal) and otherwise up-weight configurations with the largest conditional fields under the MLE. Because $\ell(\beta)$ is concave in $\beta$ for fixed $\{\Theta_j^{(r)},h_j^{(r)}\}$ (it is the log-likelihood of an exponential-family model in its natural parameter), Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} has at most one interior root, which bounded Newton or bisection on $[0.01,50]$ finds directly.

No code in the audited repository implemented Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} (Sec.~\ref{sec:code-gaps}, item T1); the archived joint $(v^{(r)},h^{(r)})$ samples themselves are also not retained for any real annealer run, so a direct re-analysis on recorded QPU data is not possible. A separate, pre-existing validation study (\texttt{scripts/exper/cem\_validation\_sweep.py}) did, however, leave three trained-RBM checkpoints on disk ($N=8$, $h=1.0$, early/mid/late training stages) together with the classical Gibbs sampler used to test $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{LS}}$ against an exact visible-marginal reference $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}$. That Gibbs sampler targets the RBM's own conditionals with no rescaling (confirmed by reading \texttt{src/sampler.py}'s \texttt{\_gibbs\_sample}: $p(h\mid v)=\sigma(2(b+Wv))$, no $\beta_x$ anywhere), so its true generating $\beta$ is exactly $1$, not merely estimated -- letting both estimators be scored against a known answer.

We implemented Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} and drew $150$ independent fresh samples per checkpoint from the surviving models (CPU only; $N=8$ makes this sub-second work). Figure~\ref{fig:mle-vs-ls} shows the result: both estimators are essentially unbiased at all three training stages (bias $\le0.01$ in $\beta$), and $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$ has a modest but consistent variance reduction over $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{LS}}$ once the network is at least partially trained -- standard deviation $0.042$ vs.\ $0.053$ at ``mid'' and $0.046$ vs.\ $0.056$ at ``late'' -- with no detectable difference at the untrained ``early'' checkpoint. This is a modest effect, not the large gain one might hope for, and it tests only the well-specified case (samples genuinely drawn from the model's own Gibbs law at $\beta=1$); it says nothing about the misspecified regime a real annealer's non-Gibbs output represents, which is what Sec.~\ref{sec:cem-theory} still leaves open.

\paragraph{Status.} \textbf{T1: partially closed.} The two estimators are now numerically compared, with a real (if modest) efficiency result, on a controlled synthetic reference. What remains open, and is unchanged from before: no real annealer run has ever had its raw joint samples logged, so whether $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$'s advantage over $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{LS}}$ persists -- or reverses -- under actual QPU non-Gibbs distortion is still untested and requires new instrumented hardware runs.

\subsection{What agreement of hidden conditionals does not imply}
\label{sec:cem-limits}

Write $m_j(v)=\mathbb E_q[h_j\mid v]$ for the sampler's true hidden conditional mean. The expected per-configuration loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cem-ls} decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathbb E_q[\mathcal L(\beta)]}{S}=\sum_j\mathbb E_{q(v)}\Big[\operatorname{Var}_q(h_j\mid v)+[m_j(v)-\tanh(\beta\phi_j(v))]^2\Big].
\label{eq:conditionalproof}
\end{equation}
If the hidden conditionals are exactly correct at some $\beta_0$, that value minimizes Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditionalproof} \emph{regardless of the visible marginal $q(v)$}. A minimal counterexample makes this concrete: one visible and one hidden spin, $a=0$, $b=0.2$, $W=0.4$, target $\beta=1$, so the two conditional fields are $\mp0.2,\allowbreak 0.6$ depending on $v=\mp1$. A sampler that places all visible weight on $v=-1$ but draws $h$ from the correct conditional at that $v$ reproduces $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{LS}}=1$ exactly, while its visible total-variation error is
\begin{equation}
\DTV(q(v),\pi_\theta(v))=\frac{\cosh(0.6)}{\cosh(-0.2)+\cosh(0.6)}\approx0.5375.
\label{eq:counterexample}
\end{equation}
Any pooled-CEM estimate -- least-squares or MLE -- is therefore a diagnostic of the hidden-layer response scale, not a certificate of the full visible distribution; Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration} reports the residual visible-distribution error directly rather than inferring it from $\widehat\beta$ alone.

\subsection{Statistical properties: what remains to be established}
\label{sec:cem-theory}

The evaluation that motivated this paper asked for a genuine theoretical treatment of the estimator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}, beyond exhibiting the score equation. This subsection records, as explicit open items rather than folding them silently into future work, what such a treatment requires.

\missing{\textbf{Identifiability and consistency under the ideal Gibbs model.} Show that if $(v^{(r)},h^{(r)})_{r=1}^S$ are i.i.d.\ draws from Eq.~\eqref{eq:tempered} at true parameter $\beta_0$, then $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}\to\beta_0$ as $S\to\infty$. This follows from standard exponential-family M-estimation theory (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cem-mle} is a one-parameter canonical exponential family in $\beta$ conditional on $\{\Theta_j^{(r)}\}$) but has not been written out or numerically checked against simulated data in this project.}

\missing{\textbf{Fisher information / asymptotic variance.} The conditional Fisher information is $I(\beta)=\sum_{r,j}(\Theta_j^{(r)})^2\,\mathrm{sech}^2(\beta\Theta_j^{(r)})$, giving asymptotic variance $I(\beta)^{-1}$ under i.i.d.\ sampling. A worked comparison of this variance to the least-squares estimator's asymptotic variance (via the sandwich formula, since Eq.~\eqref{eq:cem-ls} is not the true likelihood) is not yet computed.}

\missing{\textbf{Block bootstrap / sandwich variance under correlated QPU reads.} Successive anneals from the same problem instance are not necessarily independent (shared programming, drift). A block bootstrap over SR iterations, or a sandwich estimator using the empirical score covariance, would give a defensible confidence interval for $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$; no such interval is currently reported for any $\widehat\beta$ in this project.}

\missing{\textbf{Misspecification when one scalar $\beta$ is insufficient.} If the true sampler departs from Eq.~\eqref{eq:tempered} (e.g.\ has qubit- or chain-dependent effective temperatures), $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}}$ converges to a well-defined pseudo-true value under standard M-estimation theory, but the size of the resulting misspecification bias is not quantified here. A diagnostic residual (e.g.\ a per-$j$ or per-chain breakdown of Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditionalproof}) is a natural next step.}

\missing{\textbf{A bound linking $|\widehat\beta-\beta_0|$ to bias in the SR force vector.} Because $\betaeff$ enters training only through the samples used to form $F_k=\avg{O_k\Eloc}-\avg{O_k}\avg{\Eloc}$, a first-order sensitivity bound $\|\delta F\|\lesssim c(\theta)\,|\widehat\beta-\beta_0|$ would connect the thermometry error directly to the optimization it feeds. Deriving $c(\theta)$ and checking it numerically against the archived training trajectories is unstarted.}
